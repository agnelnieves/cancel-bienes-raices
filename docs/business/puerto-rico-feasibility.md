# Puerto Rico feasibility and three-year projections

> **Research date:** July 29, 2026  
> **Product:** Cancel Bienes Raíces  
> **Scope:** Puerto Rico only; United States expansion excluded  
> **Verdict:** **Conditional go for a paid, controlled beta. No-go for a broad
> public data launch until commercial data rights and claim substantiation are
> complete.**

## Executive view

The product is feasible if it wins as a **trusted investor workflow built on
verified local data and Christopher's distribution**. It is much less feasible
as a generic Puerto Rico property-data portal: RED Atlas is already pursuing
that position with deeper structured data and a consumer/investor product.

The strongest evidence in favor:

- the current demo solves a coherent workflow—comparables, underwriting,
  saved analysis, deal pipeline and a Spanish-first Copilot—instead of merely
  listing properties;
- Christopher reports an immediately reachable audience of approximately
  1,600 free-community members and 200 private-community members;
- Puerto Rico's information is fragmented enough that provenance, recency and
  workflow have real value;
- home prices have moved sharply: the developmental FHFA Puerto Rico
  purchase-only index rose about 16.3% year over year and 44.1% over five years
  through 2026 Q1, based on the seasonally adjusted series;
- the STR market is large and volatile, increasing the value of sober
  underwriting rather than optimistic deal marketing.

The strongest evidence against:

- commercial, customer-facing data rights are not yet secured;
- RED Atlas is a direct, well-funded-looking substitute, not merely an API
  vendor;
- the current landing presents mock-data metrics and testimonials as live
  facts;
- Puerto Rico's population is declining and household income is low, which
  limits a broad consumer subscription;
- a bundled “Community included” tier may drive usage without creating
  incremental platform revenue;
- collecting and verifying exclusive cash sales is an operations business as
  much as a software feature.

This is a good **wedge business** and a plausible profitable niche. The
research does not yet support treating it as a venture-scale outcome or
projecting mainland expansion.

## What must be true

The business works only if these five hypotheses survive a paid beta:

1. Investors will pay $30 every month, not only when actively buying a
   property.
2. The platform returns meaningfully better or faster answers than a realtor,
   spreadsheet, RED Atlas, or existing portal.
3. The contributor network can create a recent, verifiable data advantage at
   less than roughly 20%-25% of subscription revenue.
4. Christopher's audience converts because it trusts the product, not merely
   because it is included at no incremental cost.
5. Users return to a recurring workflow—watchlists, pipeline, updates,
   community and deal review—between transactions.

## Puerto Rico market evidence

### A real housing market, but not a mass-market SaaS audience

The Census Bureau estimates Puerto Rico's July 2025 population at 3,184,835,
down 3.1% from the 2020 base. It reports 1,290,866 households, 67.9%
owner-occupancy, a $131,500 median owner-occupied home value, $578 median
gross rent, and 80.6% broadband adoption. Median household income is $26,297
and 37.3% of people are below the poverty line.

Implications:

- 1.29M households is not the total addressable customer count; most are not
  active real-estate investors.
- $360/year is meaningful against local median income, so the product must
  demonstrate a concrete return and should preserve a useful free tier.
- broadband is sufficient for a web product, but mobile UX and Spanish-first
  explanations matter.
- falling population and a 24.6% 65+ share weaken a simple
  “Puerto Rico housing always grows” thesis.

The Census estimates the population fell 17,686 from July 2024 to July 2025.
This does not eliminate investor activity, but it makes neighborhood-level
demand, liquidity, migration, age, insurance, and exit assumptions essential.

### Prices have risen faster than the general story suggests

FHFA publishes a developmental Puerto Rico index, separate from its state
ranking. The seasonally adjusted purchase-only series moved from 233.23 in
2025 Q1 to 271.30 in 2026 Q1:

- **+16.3% year over year**
- **+44.1% versus 2021 Q1**

The all-transactions developmental index shows a similar +16.0% year-over-year
move. These are index changes, not the median price, and FHFA explicitly calls
the Puerto Rico series developmental. Mortgage-linked source data can also
underrepresent parts of a cash-heavy market.

Implications:

- recent comps and observed dates matter enormously;
- older appraisals, CRIM values, and stale portal listings are dangerous inputs;
- fast appreciation helps demand for analysis but hurts affordability and can
  make backward-looking comps misleading;
- this is strong support for the product problem, not proof that every deal is
  attractive.

### STR creates demand for calculators—and a warning against hype

Discover Puerto Rico, using AirDNA/STR data, reported 5.7M available rental
nights in 2024 (+10%) and more than 3M booked nights. By 2025, total lodging
supply had expanded from nearly 8M nights in 2019 to 12.5M; rentals became 53%
of supply.

The more important 2026 finding is that independent-rental RevPAR fell from a
2021 high of $148 to **$111 in 2025** as supply expanded, while hotels reached
$213. Early 2026 bookings were healthy, but AirDNA characterized 2026 as
normalization, with national occupancy expected to dip around 1% as listing
supply grew.

Implications:

- the Airbnb calculator is commercially relevant;
- revenue estimates need seasonality, insurance, utilities, cleaning,
  management, permits/taxes, capex and conservative occupancy;
- marketing should lead with “avoid a bad deal,” not guaranteed upside;
- frequently refreshed local ADR/occupancy data is a paid-data problem of its
  own and should not be represented as live until licensed.

### The macro backdrop is slow, not catastrophic

The Puerto Rico Planning Board's published 2025-2026 scenarios projected real
GNP growth of:

| Scenario | FY2025 | FY2026 |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Pessimistic | -1.0% | -0.4% |
| Base | +1.1% | +0.5% |
| Optimistic | +3.2% | +1.6% |

These are macro context, not this product's scenarios. They support a
conservative conclusion: the business should be built around an existing warm
audience and provable investor ROI, not assumed island-wide software growth.

## Customer and reachable market

The best launch market is bottom-up, not a percentage of all Puerto Rico
households.

Founder-provided, not independently verified:

- about **1,600** people in the free community;
- about **200** people in the private community;
- a planning assumption that **50** are active enough to support a $30/month
  offer.

Applied to the combined 1,800-person warm reach:

| Paid-equivalent conversion | Seats | MRR at $30 | ARR |
| ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 3% | 54 | $1,620 | $19,440 |
| 5% | 90 | $2,700 | $32,400 |
| 10% | 180 | $5,400 | $64,800 |
| 20% | 360 | $10,800 | $129,600 |

The key term is **paid-equivalent**. If the product is included with an
existing community subscription, the venture needs a written per-seat
allocation or revenue share. Otherwise those members are product users but
produce $0 of incremental platform revenue.

Useful scale markers at the current price:

- $100K ARR requires 278 average paid subscribers;
- $300K ARR requires 834;
- $1M ARR requires 2,778.

The first two are plausible in Puerto Rico if retention and referrals work.
The third is the bull case and should not be assumed from the initial audience.

## Competitive reality

### RED Atlas changes the thesis

RED Atlas's Puerto Rico site advertises 50,000+ listings, transaction records,
ownership, documents, valuations, investor/developer workflows, and future
portfolio and local-market reporting. Its API claims 1.3M+ Puerto Rico
properties and full endpoint coverage.

Cancel Bienes Raíces should not claim to be the only Puerto Rico data platform.
The defensible position is:

> The action layer for Christopher's investor community: trusted cash-deal
> evidence, transparent comps, underwriting, next steps and collaboration in
> Puerto Rican Spanish.

That is meaningfully different if the proprietary contributor dataset exists.
Without it, the product is a polished workflow sitting on other parties' data,
which is useful but easier to copy.

RED Atlas is also a difficult launch supplier. Its standard API terms are
internal-use only and prohibit redistribution in a third-party product,
commercial derived works, and competitive use without a separate written
agreement. Treat any authorized use as a strategic partnership with negotiated
economics, not a normal $750 API subscription.

### MLS and portal data are licensed assets

Stellar MLS now serves Puerto Rico and offers IDX/data services. Its own
materials explain that brokers control listing-feed distribution. A broker or
agent account does not authorize copying listings or sold data into this paid
platform. Consumer listing portals also have copyright and terms restrictions.

The feasibility study assumes one of these launch strategies:

1. **Preferred:** contributor-owned cash deals + written broker/MLS/data
   licenses + public aggregate data;
2. **Acceptable:** a signed RED Atlas commercialization agreement whose price
   works at fewer than 500 paid users;
3. **Not acceptable:** scraping or copying data first and negotiating later.

## Product positioning that can win

Lead with one job:

> “Before I make an offer, show me what similar properties actually traded
> for, what assumptions make or break the return, and what to do next.”

The strongest launch loop is:

1. search or enter a property;
2. receive source-labeled, dated comparables;
3. run rental/flip/STR analysis;
4. save it to the deal pipeline;
5. ask Copilot to explain or adjust assumptions;
6. receive updates as comps, price, or assumptions change;
7. discuss a shareable analysis with the community/realtor.

The calculator, deal pipeline and Copilot make the data useful. Credit planning,
contractors, broad community replacement, and US expansion can remain in the
product, but should not distract the validation from this loop.

## Three-year scenario model

These are operating scenarios, not promises. They count standalone Pro
subscribers plus community seats only where the JV receives a documented
$30-equivalent allocation. They exclude advertising, lead sales, transaction
fees, US expansion, and future higher-priced plans.

Shared assumptions:

- $30 monthly ARPU;
- monthly billing, no annual-plan discount in the model;
- revenue shown before IVU;
- Stripe Payments + Billing + Tax modeled at 5.1% of revenue;
- AI modeled at $0.15 per paid subscriber/month;
- no RED Atlas license;
- founder-led organic/community acquisition;
- founder salaries, engineering compensation and paid media excluded from
  operating contribution.

|  | Bear | Neutral | Bull |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Paid equivalents at month 3 | 15 | 50 | 100 |
| Month 12 | 60 | 180 | 400 |
| Month 24 | 120 | 450 | 1,200 |
| Month 36 | 180 | 900 | 3,000 |
| Month-36 MRR | $5,400 | $27,000 | $90,000 |
| Month-36 ARR run rate | $64,800 | $324,000 | $1,080,000 |

### Revenue by year

The annual model uses rounded average paid-seat counts implied by each
scenario's launch ramp and year-end targets.

| Scenario | Year 1 | Year 2 | Year 3 | **Three-year revenue** |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Bear | $13,680 | $32,400 | $54,000 | **$100,080** |
| Neutral | $41,400 | $113,400 | $243,000 | **$397,800** |
| Bull | $90,000 | $288,000 | $756,000 | **$1,134,000** |

### Why the bear case happens

- customer-facing data rights take longer or cost more than expected;
- the contributor network produces incomplete/stale records;
- existing community members use the product but no revenue is allocated;
- users subscribe only during an active deal and churn afterward;
- RED Atlas or realtor workflows are “good enough”;
- a marketing claim or data error damages trust;
- price sensitivity limits conversion.

The bear case can still be a useful community product, but it does not justify
a large standalone team or expensive data license.

### Why the neutral case is reasonable

- 50 launch seats are truly paid-equivalent;
- the warm audience converts in the mid-single to low-double digits over time;
- core-zone data is consistently better/faster for the investment workflow;
- reminders, saved deals and community analysis create recurring value;
- contributor/data operations stay below about 25% of revenue;
- organic referrals offset normal churn.

At 900 paid equivalents, the business reaches a $324K ARR run rate and should
support a small focused team if the data operation remains efficient.

### What has to go right for the bull case

- verified cash-sale coverage becomes a recognizable local standard;
- realtor contributors receive value and create a compounding acquisition/data
  loop;
- Christopher repeatedly converts audience through real case studies;
- paid retention remains strong despite episodic property buying;
- the product adds a higher-value professional/team tier without damaging the
  $30 entry point;
- competition validates the market but does not neutralize the exclusive data.

Three thousand Puerto Rico paid equivalents is ambitious: it is 1.7 times the
current founder-reported warm audience and requires referrals, new content
reach, professional users, or new local partnerships. The bull case is not
supported by current audience size alone.

## Indicative operating contribution

From the launch-cost model, non-payroll monthly operating cost is expected to
be roughly:

- $1.3K-$2.45K around 50 paid seats;
- $2.45K-$5.05K around 200;
- $6.4K-$11.6K around 1,000.

At $30, a paid seat contributes about $28.32 after modeled Stripe and AI cost,
before platform/data/support. This suggests:

- the closed beta is close to non-payroll cash breakeven at 50 seats only at
  the efficient end of the data-operation range;
- 200 paid seats should cover a disciplined early operation;
- RED Atlas at a provisional $10K/month would consume the contribution from
  roughly 353 additional subscribers before paying any staff or other costs.

This is why the owned-data path and the community revenue allocation matter
more than Vercel or database optimization.

## Principal risks and mitigations

| Risk | Likelihood / impact | Mitigation and evidence required |
| --- | --- | --- |
| No customer-facing data rights | High / existential | Signed field-level licenses before display; counsel review |
| RED Atlas competes and/or prices supply uneconomically | High / high | Owned contributor wedge; do not depend on standard API plan |
| Cash-deal data is anecdotal or unverifiable | Medium-high / high | Evidence, attestation, QA, corrections, provenance and coverage metrics |
| Existing audience does not become incremental revenue | High / high | Paid pilot and written community-seat transfer price |
| Churn is high between deals | Medium-high / high | Watchlists, updates, pipeline, recurring market briefs, annual plan later |
| AI or calculator gives harmful certainty | Medium / high | Source citations, assumption ranges, deterministic math, disclaimers, evals |
| Credit planner triggers trust/compliance issues | Medium / high | User-entered data only; no eligibility decisions; counsel-reviewed language |
| Marketing claims are not substantiated | High / high at launch | Replace demo figures/testimonials or retain evidence for every claim |
| Security/privacy incident | Medium / high | Server-only secrets, least privilege, logs, backups, incident runbook, review |
| Founder dependency | High / medium | JV-owned accounts/data; repeatable contributor and content processes |
| Population/affordability pressure | Medium / medium | Focus on active investors/professionals and demonstrable deal ROI |

## 90-day validation plan

### Days 0-14: make the launch legal and measurable

- sign the JV/IP/cost/revenue-allocation term sheet;
- obtain written data rights for the exact beta use;
- interview 25 active/aspiring investors and 10 realtors;
- pick 4-6 launch markets from actual community activity;
- define the minimum evidence and freshness standard for a comparable;
- replace all unsupported public claims with beta/demo language;
- define PostHog events before shipping.

### Days 15-45: controlled product beta

- invite 50 users; do not make the app broadly public;
- focus onboarding on comparables → calculator → saved deal;
- load 300-500 verified transactions across the core zones, then measure query
  coverage rather than celebrating the raw count;
- use Better Auth, real server state and entitlement checks;
- run weekly data-error and Copilot-evaluation reviews;
- interview every churned or inactive user.

### Days 46-90: paid proof

- charge a real $30 price to at least 25-50 people;
- give community members a visible internal transfer price in the JV books;
- publish only genuine, consented case studies;
- compare activation/retention by source and investor experience;
- decide go, narrow, or stop using the gates below.

## Go/no-go gates after 90 days

Proceed to public Puerto Rico launch only if:

- at least 25-50 users pay or are backed by a real JV seat allocation;
- at least 40% of paid users analyze two or more properties in a month;
- 60-day paid retention is at least 70%, with a path to monthly revenue churn
  below 8%;
- at least 40% of surveyed target users say they would be very disappointed to
  lose the product, or the qualitative evidence is equivalently strong;
- 90%+ of displayed proprietary comps meet the evidence/provenance standard;
- the most common core-zone searches return useful recent comps;
- organic/customer-acquisition payback is below three months;
- data verification can stay below 25% of subscription revenue at scale;
- there is no unresolved data-license, privacy, security or tax blocker.

Stop or narrow the product if users love the education/community but do not
pay for the data workflow, or if the only viable data agreement requires an
enterprise price before the business has several hundred retained subscribers.

## Recommendation

Launch a **paid Puerto Rico beta**, not a full public portal.

Use Supabase, Better Auth, Stripe, Resend, MapTiler and AI Gateway. Put the
venture's money into written data rights, a verified local corpus, truth in
marketing, and retention—not into an enterprise data contract before proof.

The product has a credible path to a profitable Puerto Rico niche and a
neutral-case $324K ARR run rate by month 36. The bull case can reach about
$1.08M ARR, but only after the data/community loop expands materially beyond
the current audience. The data-rights and revenue-allocation decisions should
be made before another public claim or subscription is launched.
