Quick answer: **don't sell revenue share alone — sell partnership. Ask for 25% of the business, plus a monthly cut, plus costs covered.**

The actual pitch numbers:

1. **25% equity in the JV** (the entity that owns the product, code, and brand). Open the negotiation at 30%, settle at 25%. Don't go below 20% under any structure — below that you're a contractor with extra steps. His brief already anchors at 80/20, so you're negotiating upward from his opening, not inventing a number.

2. **Same percentage as monthly revenue distribution** — 25% of *net product revenue*: subscriptions now, plus the future streams from his own plan (realtor ads, lender leads, US expansion). Written into the agreement, paid monthly, with both of you having live access to Stripe/metrics.

3. **All costs on him** — Vercel, Supabase, Better Auth, Stripe fees, data/API costs, realtor cash-deal incentives. Non-negotiable; he's already offering it.

4. **A small bridge while revenue is tiny** — at his own projections, 25% is $375/mo in month 3 and ~$3K/mo in month 12. Ask for *one* of these, in exchange for dropping from 30 to 25%: either $2–3K upfront "setup payment," or a $500–750/mo maintenance retainer that starts once MRR passes $2,500.

5. **Protections in writing** (more valuable than the percentage): code/IP owned by the JV entity (not by him, not by you), a buyout formula if either exits (e.g., 3× trailing 12-month distributions), vesting/inactivity clauses both ways, and your credit as product owner.

Why equity matters: if this works, the cash-deal dataset + platform becomes an asset someone might acquire or that later raises money. 25% of *that* is worth more than 25% of monthly revenue ever will be — and it's the one thing revenue share alone never pays you for his distribution scaling your work.

How to frame it to him: "You own distribution, data, and sales. I own the product end to end — strategy, design, code, AI, maintenance. I'll take no meaningful money upfront. I want 25% of the business we build, costs on you, terms in writing." If he counters at 20%, take 20% equity + 25% revenue share, or 20/20 + the retainer. If he resists equity entirely, that's your signal to charge contractor rates instead — roughly $15–25K for what you just built — because then it's not a partnership.
